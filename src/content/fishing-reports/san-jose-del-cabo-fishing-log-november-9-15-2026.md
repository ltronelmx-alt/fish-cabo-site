**Yellowfin tuna offered the clearest offshore target around San Jose del Cabo during November 9–15, but the week's results were far from uniform.** Larger fish were concentrated around Outer Gordo, while the number of wahoo and dorado brought in remained low despite searches across a wide stretch of coast.

For anglers planning a trip, the practical message is to stay adaptable. Tuna presentations accounted for the strongest opportunities, but the same period also brought a few billfish hookups and limited action over rocky bottom.

## Weather and Ocean Conditions

Water temperature was the clearest measurable change during the week. Readings averaged about **84°F** and dipped to about **83°F** later in the period. Live sardines also remained in short supply, making it useful to have alternatives ready rather than build a plan around one bait.

No numerical wind, swell, or current readings were provided for this period, so the temperature trend should not be mistaken for a complete marine forecast. Check local conditions close to departure before choosing a route or fishing method.

## Yellowfin Tuna: Bigger Fish Favored Outer Gordo

Outer Gordo accounted for most of the larger yellowfin tuna reported during the week. The common reported size range was about **40–90 lb**. A smaller group fell in a higher band of about **145–240 lb**, topped by one tuna at about **240 lb**.

The bite was inconsistent. Some morning outings produced one or two quality fish, while others finished without a tuna. Later-afternoon activity also offered opportunities, particularly around the offshore grounds.

Bait choice varied with the fish and what was available. Larger tuna were taken on a mix of bulito, live skipjack, squid strips, chiwili, and cut skipjack. Very small tuna also appeared as anglers looked for bait, taking compact hoochies or jigs; Rapalas accounted for some fish encountered during wahoo searches.

With live sardines scarce, carrying several proven alternatives made more sense than relying on a single presentation. The stronger reports from Outer Gordo do not mean that every trip there will find feeding tuna; the daily bite remained unpredictable.

## Wahoo, Dorado and Billfish: Scattered Results

Wahoo searches extended from Cardon and El 25 through Inner Gordo, Iman, La Fortuna, San Luis, and Vinorama. The total was still modest: **two wahoo and one dorado** landed across the reported activity. The two wahoo came on separate live-bait presentations: chiwili and a smaller skipjack used live.

Several strikes at Inner Gordo did not convert. Anglers were slow-trolling skipjack or presenting squid strips without wire leaders while also looking for tuna. For anyone mixing tuna and wahoo tactics, that is a useful reminder to discuss bite protection before lines go in.

The weekend also brought striped marlin and sailfish hookups around Inner Gordo while squid strips and dead sardines were drifted. These were reported hookups, not a confirmed tally of fish landed.

## Bottom Fishing: A Limited Backup

Fishing over rocky structure produced only a small number of grouper, snapper, and triggerfish. Activity remained subdued, with catches coming from anglers trying different locations and bait presentations rather than from a clearly established hot spot.

Bottom fishing therefore remained an option, but the week's observations do not support treating it as a consistently productive alternative to tuna.

## Fishing Outlook

Yellowfin tuna remain the most compelling target in the available regional picture, with Outer Gordo worth considering when the aim is a larger fish. The uneven bite means that a successful morning should not be treated as a reliable daily pattern, and a flexible bait plan is important while live sardines are limited.

Wahoo should be approached as a secondary possibility rather than an assured catch. If targeting them while also fishing for tuna, review the leader setup with the crew so the tackle suits both species. Striped marlin and sailfish add another possibility around Inner Gordo, although the recent observations describe hookups rather than consistent landings.

Bottom fishing can provide another option over structure, but recent activity was light. The best plan is to make the day's choice using current water conditions, bait availability, and the latest local observations.
