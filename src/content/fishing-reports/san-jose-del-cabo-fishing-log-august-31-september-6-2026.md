<h1>San Jose del Cabo Fishing Log — August 31–September 6, 2026</h1>



<a href="/fishing-reports/category/San%20Jose%20del%20Cabo%20Fishing%20Log">San Jose del Cabo Fishing Log</a>


<time datetime="2026-09-06">
Sep 6
</time>
Written by <a href="/fishing-reports?author=69113037595a20384a76acde">EXOTIC FISH-CABO</a>





<h1><strong>Big Yellowfin Tuna at Gordo Banks, a 530-Pound Black Marlin and Hurricane Marie's Impact</strong></h1><p>Fishing around San Jose del Cabo during August 31–September 6, 2026, delivered an eventful combination of big-game fishing, productive bottom fishing and challenging weather conditions.</p><p>Inner Gordo was a highlight of the week, with four large yellowfin tuna reported early in the period, weighing between 155 and 210 pounds.</p><p>An impressive <strong>530-pound black marlin</strong> was also landed on Tuesday, September 1, providing one of the most remarkable catches of the week.</p><p>Meanwhile, Hurricane Marie brought rain, thunderstorms and changing fishing conditions, affecting the yellowfin tuna bite around Vinorama.</p><h2>Weather and Ocean Conditions</h2><p>The first week of September brought changing weather conditions to the Los Cabos region.</p><p>On Wednesday, September 2, and Thursday, September 3, the area experienced the outer effects of Hurricane Marie.</p><p>Although the hurricane did not directly strike San Jose del Cabo, the region experienced moderate winds, heavy rainfall and thunderstorms.</p><p>Cabo San Lucas suffered significant flooding during the storm, while the San Jose del Cabo area experienced less damage, although some neighborhoods temporarily lost electricity.</p><p>These conditions influenced fishing activity, particularly around Vinorama, where the yellowfin tuna bite slowed considerably following the storm.</p><p>The September 6 regional report did not provide a specific water-temperature measurement or announce a port closure for this period.</p><h2>Yellowfin Tuna — Big Fish at Inner Gordo</h2><p>Inner Gordo provided some of the week's most impressive yellowfin tuna fishing opportunities.</p><p>On Monday, August 31, and Tuesday, September 1, regional fishing reports documented <strong>four large yellowfin tuna weighing between 155 and 210 pounds</strong>.</p><p>Live bulito and skipjack were among the baitfish used while targeting larger tuna around these offshore banks.</p><p>Fishing at Inner Gordo offered the possibility of combining bottom fishing early in the morning with a later attempt at larger yellowfin tuna.</p><p>Slow trolling live bait was one of the techniques used to target these fish.</p><p>Toward the end of the week, smaller yellowfin tuna weighing approximately <strong>40–50 pounds</strong> were also reported around Inner Gordo.</p><p>These observations highlighted the variety of yellowfin sizes present in the area, although successful catches remained dependent on fishing conditions and individual encounters.</p><h2>Black Marlin — An Impressive 530-Pound Catch</h2><p>One of the most notable catches of the week occurred on Tuesday, September 1.</p><p>A <strong>530-pound black marlin</strong> was landed after taking a live skipjack intended for tuna fishing.</p><p>The fish was hooked on tackle configured for yellowfin tuna rather than a dedicated heavy marlin setup.</p><p>According to the regional report, the fight lasted approximately three hours before the marlin was successfully brought to the boat.</p><p>This exceptional encounter highlighted the potential for large billfish around the Gordo Banks during the early September period.</p><p>However, such catches are uncommon and should never be considered typical or guaranteed results.</p><h2>Yellowfin Tuna — Slower Action at Vinorama</h2><p>While large yellowfin tuna attracted attention around Inner Gordo, fishing at Vinorama followed a different pattern.</p><p>During the beginning of the week, particularly Monday and Tuesday, live sardines continued producing yellowfin tuna activity.</p><p>However, the bite slowed significantly following the passage of Hurricane Marie's outer weather bands.</p><p>Toward the end of the week, some regional fishing outings were fortunate to land only one or two tuna.</p><p>Shark interference continued to complicate fishing, with hooked yellowfin sometimes attracting sharks before they could be landed.</p><p>The difference between the stronger early-week activity and the slower post-storm results made fishing at Vinorama particularly unpredictable.</p><h2>Bottom Fishing — Cubera Snapper at Iman and Inner Gordo</h2><p>As yellowfin tuna fishing slowed around Vinorama, more anglers concentrated on bottom structure.</p><p>San Luis, Iman Bank and Inner Gordo were among the locations where bottom fishing received additional attention.</p><p>Cubera snapper were the principal bottom-fishing species highlighted in the September 6 report.</p><p>Most of the reported cubera snapper were small to medium-sized fish.</p><p><strong>Iman Bank and Inner Gordo provided the best reported bottom-fishing opportunities.</strong></p><p>Some anglers focused on bottom fishing early in the morning before shifting their attention toward larger yellowfin tuna later in the outing.</p><p>This approach provided alternatives during periods when the tuna bite was less reliable.</p><h2>Bait Availability — Sardines and Caballito</h2><p>Live bait availability continued to influence fishing opportunities during the week.</p><p>Despite the heavy rainfall associated with Hurricane Marie, regional reports indicated that sardines remained available.</p><p>This allowed fishing outings to continue using live sardines while targeting yellowfin tuna.</p><p>Caballito remained more difficult to obtain.</p><p>However, some medium-sized caballito schools were observed entering the marina channel around midday.</p><p>Live bulito and skipjack remained important alternatives for anglers pursuing larger yellowfin tuna around Inner Gordo.</p><h2>Fishing Conditions Summary</h2><ul><li><p><strong>Inner Gordo:</strong> Four large yellowfin tuna weighing 155–210 pounds reported on August 31 and September 1.</p></li><li><p><strong>Black marlin:</strong> A 530-pound fish landed on September 1 after a three-hour fight using live skipjack.</p></li><li><p><strong>Vinorama:</strong> Good yellowfin activity early in the week, followed by a significant slowdown after Hurricane Marie.</p></li><li><p><strong>Smaller yellowfin:</strong> Fish weighing approximately 40–50 pounds reported at Inner Gordo toward the end of the week.</p></li><li><p><strong>Iman Bank:</strong> Productive bottom fishing for small to medium-sized cubera snapper.</p></li><li><p><strong>San Luis:</strong> Additional attention to bottom structure as tuna fishing became less consistent.</p></li><li><p><strong>Shark activity:</strong> Continued interference with yellowfin tuna fishing around Vinorama.</p></li><li><p><strong>Bait:</strong> Sardines remained available, while caballito supplies were more limited.</p></li><li><p><strong>Weather:</strong> Hurricane Marie brought moderate winds, heavy rain and thunderstorms on September 2–3.</p></li></ul><h2>Fishing Outlook</h2><p>The August 31–September 6 fishing reports demonstrated the contrast between large offshore game-fish opportunities and changing conditions closer to shore.</p><p>Inner Gordo produced impressive yellowfin tuna reports and an exceptional black marlin catch.</p><p>Vinorama continued to hold yellowfin tuna, although the bite became less consistent following the storm.</p><p>Bottom fishing around Iman and Inner Gordo provided useful alternatives when tuna activity slowed.</p><p>With September marking an active period of the Pacific hurricane season, anglers should pay close attention to current marine forecasts and official port conditions before planning any fishing departure.</p><h2>Plan Your Private Fishing Charter</h2><p>Exotic Fish Cabo offers <strong>100% private
