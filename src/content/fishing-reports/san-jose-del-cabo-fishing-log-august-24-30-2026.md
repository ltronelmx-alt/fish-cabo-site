<h1>San Jose del Cabo Fishing Log — August 24–30, 2026</h1>



<a href="/fishing-reports/category/San%20Jose%20del%20Cabo%20Fishing%20Log">San Jose del Cabo Fishing Log</a>


<time datetime="2026-08-30">
Aug 30
</time>
Written by <a href="/fishing-reports?author=69113037595a20384a76acde">EXOTIC FISH-CABO</a>





<h1><strong>Big Yellowfin Tuna at Gordo Banks, Productive Bottom Fishing and a Powerful Torito Storm</strong></h1><p>Fishing around San Jose del Cabo during August 24–30, 2026, delivered a combination of encouraging yellowfin tuna action, productive bottom fishing and challenging weather conditions.</p><p>Vinorama remained an important location for yellowfin tuna, with most reported fish weighing between 20 and 40 pounds. Larger tuna were also present, including a 160-pound yellowfin reported at Vinorama and a 210-pound fish from the Gordo Banks.</p><p>Bottom fishing produced cubera snapper, barred snapper and grouper, particularly around Iman Bank.</p><p>However, the fishing week was significantly disrupted by a sudden and powerful Torito storm on Tuesday, August 25, bringing dangerous winds, heavy rainfall and large waves to the region.</p><h2>Weather and Ocean Conditions</h2><p>The week began with favorable fishing conditions on Monday, August 24.</p><p>Currents were manageable, allowing anglers to target both yellowfin tuna and bottom-dwelling species.</p><p>Conditions changed dramatically on Tuesday morning when a powerful local storm, commonly known as a Torito, developed over the Los Cabos region.</p><p>These storms can form rapidly and produce intense rain, severe wind gusts and electrical activity.</p><p>According to regional observations, the morning initially appeared calm. Around 9:00 a.m., dark clouds began approaching the fishing grounds.</p><p>Within approximately an hour, conditions deteriorated considerably.</p><p>Reports described winds estimated at <strong>50 knots</strong>, heavy rain and large swells. Several fishing outings were caught in dangerous conditions while attempting to return to shore.</p><p>The storm caused significant difficulties for those on the water and reinforced the importance of monitoring changing marine conditions during the Pacific tropical storm season.</p><p>No specific water-temperature measurement was provided in the weekly report.</p><h2>Yellowfin Tuna — Vinorama Remains Productive</h2><p>Vinorama continued to produce yellowfin tuna during the August 24–30 reporting period.</p><p>Many fishing outings began by targeting tuna with live sardines, a technique that had been producing consistent results throughout August.</p><p>Most reported yellowfin tuna weighed approximately <strong>20–40 pounds</strong>, offering opportunities for anglers targeting smaller and medium-sized fish.</p><p>Larger tuna were also present.</p><p>One notable catch involved a <strong>160-pound yellowfin tuna</strong> landed at Vinorama using a live bulito.</p><p>Additional larger fish were reportedly hooked in the same area, although some were lost after extended fights on lighter tackle.</p><p>These encounters demonstrated the continued presence of large yellowfin tuna alongside the more commonly caught 20–40-pound fish.</p><p>As always, the presence of feeding tuna did not guarantee successful landings.</p><h2>Big Yellowfin Tuna — Inner and Outer Gordo</h2><p>During the weekend of August 29–30, more fishing activity shifted toward Inner and Outer Gordo.</p><p>Reports of large yellowfin tuna encouraged additional outings to explore these offshore banks.</p><p>Several large fish were hooked, although only a limited number were successfully landed.</p><p>The largest reported yellowfin tuna weighed <strong>210 pounds</strong> and was caught using a live bulito.</p><p>This catch was one of the highlights of the week and confirmed that exceptionally large yellowfin continued to be encountered around the Gordo Banks.</p><p>However, fishing for these larger fish remained challenging, with successful landings less common than hookups.</p><p>Vinorama remained productive for smaller yellowfin, while Inner and Outer Gordo offered less predictable opportunities for larger fish.</p><h2>Bottom Fishing — Cubera Snapper and Barred Snapper at Iman</h2><p>Bottom fishing was particularly productive at the beginning of the week.</p><p>On Monday, August 24, favorable currents allowed some anglers to make additional drops over bottom structure using chunks of skipjack.</p><p>Iman Bank produced some of the best reported bottom-fishing action.</p><p>The main species included:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Cubera snapper</strong></p></li><li><p><strong>Barred snapper</strong></p></li></ul><p>These catches provided a productive alternative to concentrating exclusively on yellowfin tuna.</p><p>As the week progressed, changing weather and marine conditions affected fishing activity, reducing the opportunities for consistent bottom fishing.</p><h2>Grouper — A 70-Pound Catch at Vinorama</h2><p>Grouper added another highlight to the week's regional fishing reports.</p><p>A <strong>70-pound grouper</strong> was reported caught at Vinorama using a live bulito while anglers were targeting yellowfin tuna.</p><p>This unexpected catch demonstrated the variety of species that could be encountered around the area's fishing grounds.</p><p>Although grouper were not the principal target for most outings, this reported catch was among the more notable bottom-associated species landed during the week.</p><h2>The August 25 Torito Storm — A Major Safety Event</h2><p>The sudden Torito storm was one of the defining events of the August 24–30 fishing period.</p><p>Unlike a slowly approaching tropical system, this storm developed rapidly and gave anglers limited time to react.</p><p>Some fishing outings managed to return toward the marina as the clouds intensified. Others encountered extremely difficult conditions before reaching safety.</p><p>Regional reports described severe wind gusts, powerful waves and heavy rain.</p><p>The storm also caused damage to fishing equipment and vessels in the area.</p><p>Fortunately, the contemporary report indicated that the affected anglers and fishing crews survived the event.</p><p>This incident highlighted how quickly conditions can change around San Jose del Cabo, even after a calm morning.</p><p>Checking marine forecasts, following official safety guidance and responding quickly to deteriorating weather conditions remain essential parts of planning a fishing trip.</p><h2>Fishing Conditions Summary</h2><ul><li><p><strong>Vinorama:</strong> Yellowfin tuna continued to provide consistent opportunities, with most reported fish weighing approximately 20–40 pounds.</p></li><li><p><strong>Large yellowfin at Vinorama:</strong> A 160-pound tuna was reported landed on live bulito.</p></li><li><p><strong>Inner and Outer Gordo:</strong> Larger tuna were encountered during the weekend, including a reported 210-pound yellowfin.</p></li><li><p><strong>Iman Bank:</strong> Productive bottom fishing for cubera snapper and barred snapper, particularly on August 24.</p></li><li><p><strong>Grouper:</strong> A 70-pound fish was reported caught at Vinorama on live bulito.</p></li><li><p><strong>Bait:</strong> Live sardines remained productive for tuna, while bulito and chunks of skipjack were used for larger fish and bottom fishing.</p></li><li><p><strong>Weather:</strong> A powerful Torito storm struck on August 25, bringing heavy rainfall, severe wind gusts and large waves.</p></li><li><p><strong>Fishing activity:</strong> Marine conditions and relatively limited regional charter activity reduced fishing opportunities during portions of the week.</p></li></ul><h2>Fishing Outlook</h2><p>The August 24–30 fishing reports showed that yellowfin tuna continued to offer important opportunities around San Jose del Cabo.</p><p>Vinorama remained productive for medium-sized tuna, while Inner and Outer Gordo produced reports of much larger fish.</p><p>The successful bottom-fishing activity around Iman Bank also highlighted opportunities for anglers interested in snapper and grouper.</p><p>However, the sudden Torito storm demonstrated the risks associated with rapidly changing conditions during the Pacific hurricane season.</p><p>Anglers planning a fishing trip should always check current marine
