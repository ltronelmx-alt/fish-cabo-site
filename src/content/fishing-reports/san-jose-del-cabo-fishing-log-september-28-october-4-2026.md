<h1>San Jose del Cabo Fishing Log — September 28–October 4, 2026</h1>



<a href="/fishing-reports/category/San%20Jose%20del%20Cabo%20Fishing%20Log">San Jose del Cabo Fishing Log</a>


<time datetime="2026-10-04">
Oct 4
</time>
Written by <a href="/fishing-reports?author=69113037595a20384a76acde">EXOTIC FISH-CABO</a>





<h1><strong>Puerto Los Cabos Remains Closed After Consecutive Storms — No Fishing Activity This Week</strong></h1><p>Fishing around San Jose del Cabo was completely suspended during September 28–October 4, 2026, as Puerto Los Cabos remained closed following Hurricane Polo and additional storm conditions.</p><p>This was an exceptional interruption to the local fishing season, with hazardous swells, large waves, wind and rain preventing normal fishing departures.</p><p>Unlike previous weekly reports, this Fishing Log does not include yellowfin tuna, marlin, dorado or bottom-fishing catches because <strong>no fishing activity took place during the reporting period</strong>.</p><p>The port remained closed through Sunday, October 4, with fishing activities resuming the following day.</p><h2>Weather and Ocean Conditions</h2><p>The final days of September and the beginning of October were dominated by unsettled marine conditions around Los Cabos.</p><p>Hurricane Polo had already affected the region during the previous week, producing large ocean swells and waves.</p><p>Puerto Los Cabos officially closed on the evening of Thursday, September 24, with fishing departures suspended beginning September 25.</p><p>As the following week approached, additional storm activity in the Pacific created further uncertainty.</p><p>In the September 27 regional report, another developing storm system was forecast to approach the area around October 1–2.</p><p>The storm's projected path and strength were uncertain at that time, but concerns about the possibility of continued dangerous ocean conditions remained high.</p><p>A subsequent report published on October 11 confirmed that consecutive storms had kept the port closed from September 25 through October 4.</p><p>Although Los Cabos did not experience a direct hurricane strike during that closure period, the region received large swells, waves, moderate winds and substantial rainfall.</p><p>These conditions prevented fishing operations from returning to normal.</p><h2>Puerto Los Cabos — Closed Throughout the Week</h2><p>The official port closure was the defining event of the September 28–October 4 fishing period.</p><p>Local maritime authorities had already suspended departures because of the dangerous marine conditions associated with Hurricane Polo.</p><p>The continuing effects of successive storm systems extended the interruption into early October.</p><p>As a result, fishing departures remained suspended throughout the entire week.</p><p>The October 11 regional report subsequently confirmed that the port did not reopen for normal fishing operations until Monday, October 5.</p><p>This prolonged closure was described as an unusually long interruption to local fishing activity.</p><p>With the port closed, anglers were unable to pursue normal offshore, midshore or inshore fishing opportunities from Puerto Los Cabos.</p><p>The suspension applied regardless of which fishing species might otherwise have been present in the area.</p><h2>Yellowfin Tuna — No Fishing Activity Reported</h2><p>Yellowfin tuna had been a principal target around Vinorama and the Inner and Outer Gordo Banks during the preceding weeks.</p><p>However, no yellowfin tuna fishing activity was reported during September 28–October 4.</p><p>With the port closed, there were no normal fishing departures available to document tuna catches, feeding activity or daily fishing results.</p><p>It would therefore be inaccurate to describe the tuna bite as productive or unproductive during this period.</p><p><strong>No verified yellowfin tuna catches or fishing results were available for this week.</strong></p><p>The absence of catches reflects the closure of the fishing grounds to normal boat activity, not evidence that yellowfin tuna had disappeared from the region.</p><h2>Marlin, Dorado and Other Offshore Species</h2><p>Offshore fishing activity was also suspended throughout the reporting period.</p><p>The available regional reports did not document marlin, dorado or wahoo catches during September 28–October 4.</p><p>Because fishing boats were unable to operate normally, no reliable conclusions can be drawn about offshore species activity during these dates.</p><p>The previous reports had documented big-game fishing opportunities before the storm-related closure, but those observations belong to earlier fishing periods and are not presented as catches from this week.</p><h2>Bottom Fishing — Iman, San Luis and Gordo Banks</h2><p>Bottom fishing had provided some of the more encouraging opportunities before the storms, particularly around the rocky structures of Iman Bank.</p><p>Dogtooth snapper, grouper and other bottom-dwelling species had been reported during earlier September outings.</p><p>However, the closure prevented normal fishing activity at Iman, San Luis and the Gordo Banks throughout September 28–October 4.</p><p>No new bottom-fishing catches were documented for the closed period.</p><p>As with offshore tuna fishing, it is not possible to assess the actual fishing conditions or catch rates at these locations without verified fishing observations.</p><h2>Impact of Consecutive Storms on Fishing Operations</h2><p>The prolonged port closure demonstrated how quickly tropical weather can interrupt fishing activity in Los Cabos.</p><p>Even without a direct hurricane landfall, large offshore swells and dangerous wave conditions can make departures unsafe.</p><p>During this period, the immediate priority was the safety of people and vessels rather than fishing activity.</p><p>The September 27 report indicated that operators were preparing for continuing bad weather and following guidance from the port captain and maritime authorities.</p><p>The same report announced that the next regional fishing update would be delayed until October 11 because of the weather disruption.</p><p>The later report confirmed that the extended closure lasted through October 4.</p><p>Together, these updates document a complete interruption to normal fishing activities throughout this Fishing Log's reporting period.</p><h2>Fishing Conditions Summary</h2><ul><li><p><strong>Reporting period:</strong> September 28–October 4, 2026.</p></li><li><p><strong>Puerto Los Cabos:</strong> Closed throughout the entire week.</p></li><li><p><strong>Original closure:</strong> Thursday evening, September 24, following Hurricane Polo.</p></li><li><p><strong>Extended closure:</strong> September 25–October 4, confirmed in the subsequent regional report.</p></li><li><p><strong>Weather:</strong> Consecutive storm systems affected the region.</p></li><li><p><strong>Ocean conditions:</strong> Large swells, waves, moderate winds and significant rainfall.</p></li><li><p><strong>Yellowfin tuna:</strong> No fishing activity or verified catches during the closure.</p></li><li><p><strong>Marlin and dorado:</strong> No fishing activity or verified catches during the closure.</p></li><li><p><strong>Bottom fishing:</strong> No normal departures to Iman, San Luis or the Gordo Banks.</p></li><li><p><strong>Fishing results:</strong> No catch statistics available for this week.</p></li><li><p><strong>Port reopening:</strong> Fishing operations resumed Monday, October 5, 2026.</p></li></ul><h2>Fishing Outlook</h2><p>At the end of September 28–October 4, fishing activity remained suspended pending the return of safe marine conditions and official authorization to resume departures.</p><p>The subsequent regional report confirmed that fishing operations restarted on Monday, October 5.</p><p>However, conditions immediately after reopening were challenging, with murky water affecting fishing activity during the first days back on the water.</p><p>As the water gradually cleared, regional fishing opportunities began to improve.</p><p>Those observations belong to the following week's Fishing Log, covering October 5–11, 2026.</p><p>The September 28–October 4 period should therefore be understood as a complete storm-related interruption rather than an ordinary fishing week with poor catches.</p><p>During the Pacific hurricane season, anglers should always verify official port conditions and current marine forecasts before planning any offshore or inshore fishing trip.</p><h2>Plan
