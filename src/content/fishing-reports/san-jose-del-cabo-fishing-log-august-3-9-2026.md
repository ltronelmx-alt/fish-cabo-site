<h1>San Jose del Cabo Fishing Log — August 3–9, 2026</h1>



<time datetime="2026-08-09">
Aug 9
</time>
Written by <a href="/fishing-reports?author=69113037595a20384a76acde">EXOTIC FISH-CABO</a>





<p><strong>Yellowfin Tuna Action at Vinorama, Persistent Sharks and a Black Marlin Encounter at Outer Gordo</strong></p><p>Fishing around San Jose del Cabo during August 3–9, 2026, continued to revolve around yellowfin tuna, with Vinorama Bank attracting much of the regional fishing activity.</p><p>The tuna bite showed signs of improvement as the reporting period progressed, but increasing shark activity made it difficult to bring some hooked fish to the boat.</p><p>Dorado provided additional opportunities, while a large black marlin hookup at Outer Gordo added excitement to the offshore fishing picture.</p><h2>Weather and Ocean Conditions</h2><p>Following the disruption caused by Hurricane Genevieve the previous week, fishing activity continued without new storm activity reported during the first full week of August.</p><p>The regional fishing report covering August 3–11 did not document a new tropical storm affecting local fishing grounds during the previous week.</p><p>Fishing conditions nevertheless remained challenging in some areas, particularly because of increasing shark activity around the productive tuna grounds.</p><p>No verified water-temperature or water-clarity measurements were provided specifically for August 3–9.</p><h2>Yellowfin Tuna — Vinorama Bank</h2><p>Vinorama Bank remained the primary focus for many fishing outings.</p><p>Yellowfin tuna responded to live sardines, although the bite was not equally productive every day.</p><p>Early in the reporting period, some boats managed only one or two tuna during a morning outing. As the period progressed, fishing became more active, with reports indicating better opportunities to hook yellowfin.</p><p>Most of the reported tuna weighed approximately <strong>20–80 pounds</strong>, with smaller fish also present.</p><p>However, catching a tuna did not always mean successfully landing it.</p><p>Sharks were increasingly active around Vinorama, interfering with hooked fish and frequently making the final stages of a fight more difficult.</p><p>Smaller tuna generally had a better chance of reaching the boat before sharks became involved.</p><h2>Inner and Outer Gordo — Larger Tuna Opportunities</h2><p>Fishing at Inner and Outer Gordo was less consistent than at Vinorama.</p><p>The broader August 3–11 regional report described attempts to target larger yellowfin tuna at these banks, but many outings were unsuccessful.</p><p>Some tuna weighing approximately 80–120 pounds were reported during that wider reporting period. Their exact catch dates were not specified, so they cannot all be assigned confidently to August 3–9.</p><p>Overall, Vinorama provided the more consistently reported tuna activity, while the Gordo Banks offered less predictable opportunities for larger fish.</p><h2>Dorado Fishing — Vinorama to Cardon</h2><p>Dorado contributed to the variety of species reported during the period.</p><p>Some were caught on live sardines while anglers were targeting yellowfin tuna around Vinorama.</p><p>Smaller dorado were also reported while trolling artificial lures, feathers and rigged ballyhoo along the fishing grounds between Vinorama and Cardon.</p><p>These observations suggested opportunities for anglers covering different areas rather than remaining exclusively on the tuna grounds.</p><p>The precise dates of individual dorado catches were not documented.</p><h2>Black Marlin — Outer Gordo</h2><p>An offshore black marlin encounter was one of the notable highlights reported for the weekend of August 8–9.</p><p>A black marlin estimated at approximately <strong>300–400 pounds</strong> was hooked in the Outer Gordo area.</p><p>The fish was reportedly hooked on tackle intended for tuna fishing and broke free after approximately 20 minutes.</p><p>Although the marlin was not landed, the encounter demonstrated that large billfish were present around the offshore banks.</p><p>As with all offshore fishing, encountering a large fish does not guarantee a successful landing.</p><h2>Shark Activity — A Major Fishing Challenge</h2><p>One of the defining features of this fishing period was the persistent presence of sharks around Vinorama.</p><p>As yellowfin tuna activity improved, reports also described more sharks interfering with hooked fish.</p><p>Some fishing outings produced hookups without equivalent numbers of landed tuna.</p><p>This situation made fishing results particularly unpredictable, even when anglers found active yellowfin.</p><p>Shark interference remained an important factor when evaluating the overall fishing conditions.</p><h2>Fishing Conditions Summary</h2><ul><li><p><strong>Vinorama Bank:</strong> Yellowfin tuna remained the principal target, with reported fish commonly weighing 20–80 pounds.</p></li><li><p><strong>Live bait:</strong> Sardines were used successfully for yellowfin tuna and occasional dorado.</p></li><li><p><strong>Shark activity:</strong> Increasing interference reduced the number of tuna successfully landed.</p></li><li><p><strong>Inner and Outer Gordo:</strong> Less consistent tuna fishing, with larger fish reported during the broader August 3–11 period.</p></li><li><p><strong>Dorado:</strong> Occasional activity around Vinorama and along trolling grounds toward Cardon.</p></li><li><p><strong>Black marlin:</strong> A fish estimated at 300–400 pounds was hooked and lost at Outer Gordo during the August 8–9 weekend.</p></li><li><p><strong>Weather:</strong> No new storm activity was reported for the first full week of August.</p></li></ul><h2>Fishing Outlook</h2><p>As August progressed, yellowfin tuna remained the main focus of fishing activity around San Jose del Cabo.</p><p>Vinorama continued to offer opportunities, but shark interference and the unpredictable behavior of feeding tuna meant that daily results could vary substantially.</p><p>The presence of larger tuna around the Gordo Banks and the reported black marlin encounter also highlighted the potential for offshore big-game fishing.</p><p>During the Pacific hurricane season, checking current port conditions and marine
