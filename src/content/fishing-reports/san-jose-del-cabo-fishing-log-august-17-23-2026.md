<h1>San Jose del Cabo Fishing Log — August 17–23, 2026</h1>



<a href="/fishing-reports/category/San%20Jose%20del%20Cabo%20Fishing%20Log">San Jose del Cabo Fishing Log</a>


<time datetime="2026-08-23">
Aug 23
</time>
Written by <a href="/fishing-reports?author=69113037595a20384a76acde">EXOTIC FISH-CABO</a>





<h1><strong>Yellowfin Tuna Remain the Main Target at Vinorama, with a 200-Pound Catch Reported</strong></h1><p>Fishing around San Jose del Cabo during August 17–23, 2026, continued to focus heavily on yellowfin tuna, particularly around the productive waters of Vinorama.</p><p>Live sardines remained available, supporting consistent tuna fishing opportunities despite persistent shark interference.</p><p>Most reported yellowfin tuna weighed between 20 and 60 pounds, although a much larger 200-pound tuna was also landed during the week.</p><p>Scattered dorado schools, occasional dogtooth snapper and reports of larger tuna around the Gordo Banks added variety to the regional fishing picture.</p><h2>Weather and Ocean Conditions</h2><p>The week brought very warm, humid summer weather around San Jose del Cabo, accompanied by increasing cloud cover and scattered rain.</p><p>With late August approaching the peak of the Pacific hurricane season, tropical weather developments remained an important consideration.</p><p>The regional report dated August 23 described several potential storm systems developing in the Pacific, with the nearest forecast to approach during the first days of September.</p><p>However, storm tracks were changing frequently, and no specific impact could be confirmed at the time.</p><p>No measured water-temperature reading was provided in the report for this week.</p><h2>Yellowfin Tuna — Vinorama Continues to Produce</h2><p>Vinorama remained the main focus of fishing activity during August 17–23.</p><p>Yellowfin tuna continued responding to live sardines, which were available from bait suppliers near the marina entrance.</p><p>On productive days, some regional fishing outings reported landing <strong>four to five yellowfin tuna</strong>, most commonly in the <strong>20–30-pound range</strong>.</p><p>Other days were considerably slower, with only one or two tuna landed during a morning outing.</p><p>A smaller number of larger yellowfin weighing approximately <strong>50–60 pounds</strong> were also reported from the same area.</p><p>These results highlighted the inconsistent nature of the tuna bite: fish were present, but daily success could vary significantly.</p><h2>Big Yellowfin Tuna — A 200-Pound Fish at Vinorama</h2><p>One of the most notable catches reported during the week occurred on Friday, August 21.</p><p>A <strong>200-pound yellowfin tuna</strong> was landed at Vinorama using a live skipjack.</p><p>That same day, another large tuna was hooked on skipjack but lost after an approximately one-hour fight.</p><p>These encounters demonstrated the presence of much larger yellowfin tuna alongside the more commonly reported 20–60-pound fish.</p><p>Fishing for tuna of this size requires suitable equipment and patience, but even a successful hookup does not guarantee a landing.</p><h2>Inner and Outer Gordo — Additional Big Tuna Activity</h2><p>Larger yellowfin tuna were also reported around Inner and Outer Gordo during the week.</p><p>Regional observations described big tuna taken using live skipjack and bulito.</p><p>Although the report did not provide specific weights or totals for these catches, it indicated that large yellowfin were present around both fishing areas.</p><p>Vinorama nevertheless remained the more consistently productive location for the majority of fishing outings.</p><h2>Shark Activity — An Ongoing Challenge</h2><p>Shark interference remained one of the defining fishing challenges around Vinorama.</p><p>Even when yellowfin tuna were feeding, sharks sometimes made it difficult to bring hooked fish to the boat.</p><p>As a result, the number of tuna hooked did not always translate into an equivalent number of successful landings.</p><p>The continuing presence of sharks made fishing results less predictable, particularly when targeting larger yellowfin.</p><h2>Dorado and Dogtooth Snapper</h2><p>Dorado activity was also observed around Vinorama toward the end of the week.</p><p>Several schools of small dorado were reported during the weekend of August 22–23.</p><p>However, most of these fish were considered too small to keep, so the sightings did not represent a significant harvest of dorado.</p><p>Occasional dogtooth snapper were also reported around the same offshore high spots where anglers were drifting for yellowfin tuna.</p><p>These catches provided additional variety, although yellowfin tuna remained the principal target.</p><h2>Inshore Fishing — Limited Activity</h2><p>Inshore fishing received relatively little attention during this reporting period.</p><p>As August progressed, the regional report described a seasonal decline in the amount of nearshore fishing activity.</p><p>Rather than focusing on roosterfish or other inshore species, most outings concentrated on yellowfin tuna around Vinorama and the Gordo Banks.</p><p>The report did not provide enough confirmed inshore catch details to establish a reliable catch pattern for the week.</p><h2>Fishing Conditions Summary</h2><ul><li><p><strong>Vinorama:</strong> Yellowfin tuna remained the principal target, with most reported fish weighing 20–30 pounds.</p></li><li><p><strong>Larger yellowfin:</strong> Some tuna weighing 50–60 pounds were reported at Vinorama.</p></li><li><p><strong>Big tuna highlight:</strong> A 200-pound yellowfin was reported landed at Vinorama on August 21 using live skipjack.</p></li><li><p><strong>Inner and Outer Gordo:</strong> Additional large yellowfin tuna reported on live skipjack and bulito.</p></li><li><p><strong>Live bait:</strong> Sardines remained available near the marina entrance.</p></li><li><p><strong>Shark activity:</strong> Continued interference during yellowfin tuna fights.</p></li><li><p><strong>Dorado:</strong> Schools of small fish observed around Vinorama during the weekend, with few considered suitable to keep.</p></li><li><p><strong>Dogtooth snapper:</strong> Occasional catches around offshore high spots.</p></li><li><p><strong>Weather:</strong> Humid conditions, scattered rain and ongoing monitoring of Pacific storm developments.</p></li></ul><h2>Fishing Outlook</h2><p>The August 17–23 fishing reports showed that yellowfin tuna continued to provide the main opportunities around San Jose del Cabo.</p><p>Vinorama offered relatively consistent action compared with other fishing locations, although catches varied considerably between outings.</p><p>The reported 200-pound yellowfin and additional large fish around the Gordo Banks highlighted the potential for bigger tuna.</p><p>However, persistent sharks and changing tropical weather conditions remained important factors.</p><p>With the Pacific hurricane season approaching its peak, anglers should always verify current marine forecasts and port conditions before planning a fishing trip.</p><h2>Plan Your Private Fishing Charter</h2><p>Exotic Fish Cabo
