**Big Yellowfin Tuna and Improving Wahoo Action as November Begins in San Jose del Cabo**

The final week of October brought exciting offshore fishing opportunities around San Jose del Cabo, with impressive yellowfin tuna activity at the Gordo Banks and encouraging signs of improving wahoo fishing along the Sea of Cortez coastline.

Although changing weather conditions and temporary port closures interrupted fishing activity during the week, anglers who made it offshore encountered opportunities for quality catches.

Yellowfin tuna remained the standout species, with several impressive fish reported from the offshore banks. Wahoo activity also showed positive signs, particularly around the eastern fishing grounds.

As November begins, San Jose del Cabo continues to offer exciting possibilities for anglers targeting powerful offshore game fish.

## Weather and Ocean Conditions

Weather conditions played an important role during the week of October 26 through November 1.

Large swells and heavy rainfall affected offshore fishing activity, resulting in temporary closures of Puerto Los Cabos on Wednesday and Thursday.

Fishing operations resumed Friday morning, although additional rainfall forced some boats to return earlier than planned.

Conditions varied along the coastline, with certain fishing areas farther east experiencing less rainfall than the immediate San Jose del Cabo region.

Water temperatures showed a gradual cooling trend:

- **Inner and Outer Gordo Banks:** Approximately 87–88°F
- **Vinorama, San Luis, and Iman Bank:** Approximately 86–87°F

Despite the gradual temperature decrease, offshore waters remained relatively warm for the season.

These changing conditions could influence bait movement, game fish concentrations, and fishing techniques as November progresses.

Anglers planning a fishing charter should always consider current marine forecasts, local port conditions, and sea conditions before departure.

## Yellowfin Tuna – Impressive Action at Gordo Banks

Yellowfin tuna continued to provide some of the most exciting offshore opportunities during the final week of October.

The Inner and Outer Gordo Banks remained important fishing grounds for anglers searching for larger tuna.

Many reported yellowfin tuna weighed approximately **50–100 lb**, while several significantly larger fish were reported in the **100–230 lb** range.

The larger tuna required patience, careful bait presentation, and persistence.

Some productive outings resulted in multiple quality yellowfin tuna, although catches varied considerably between boats and fishing days.

Different natural bait presentations produced opportunities throughout the week, including:

- Dead sardines
- Squid
- Skipjack chunks
- Live chiwili
- Live skipjack

The availability of suitable bait and the behavior of feeding tuna played important roles in fishing success.

For anglers hoping to experience the power of a large yellowfin tuna, the Gordo Banks remained among the most promising locations near San Jose del Cabo.

## Vinorama, San Luis and Iman Bank – Variable Tuna Activity

Farther east, fishing conditions were less consistent.

Vinorama, San Luis, and Iman Bank continued to produce occasional yellowfin tuna, but the action was generally less reliable than at the Gordo Banks.

Some fishing outings produced only one or two tuna, while others experienced limited activity.

This difference between fishing grounds highlights the importance of adapting to changing offshore conditions.

Water temperatures, currents, bait availability, and daily fish movements can all influence which areas produce the best opportunities.

For private fishing charters departing from Puerto Los Cabos Marina, selecting fishing grounds based on current conditions remains an important part of planning a productive offshore experience.

## Wahoo Fishing – Improving Opportunities Along the Coast

Wahoo fishing showed encouraging signs of improvement during the reporting period.

Increased wahoo activity was observed around Vinorama, San Luis, and Iman Bank, where anglers encountered these fast-moving predators while targeting offshore species.

Several reported wahoo were medium-sized, with occasional larger fish weighing approximately **35–40 lb**.

Trolling diving lures and rigged ballyhoo were among the productive techniques observed during the week.

Known for explosive strikes, remarkable speed, and aggressive runs, wahoo remain one of the most exciting offshore species available around Los Cabos.

As water temperatures gradually cool, wahoo activity may become increasingly interesting for anglers planning early November fishing trips.

However, fishing conditions can change rapidly, and no particular species or catch can be guaranteed.

## Dorado and Bottom Fishing Activity

Dorado fishing remained relatively slow during the week.

Only scattered smaller dorado were reported, and there was no indication of widespread or consistent dorado activity across the main fishing grounds.

Bottom fishing also produced limited results compared with stronger periods earlier in the season.

Nevertheless, anglers fishing rocky structures around Iman Bank, San Luis, and Vinorama continued to encounter occasional bottom-dwelling species, including:

- Dogtooth snapper
- Barred snapper
- Grouper

Chunks of skipjack were among the natural bait presentations used when targeting bottom fish.

Although offshore tuna and wahoo received most of the attention, bottom fishing remained an alternative depending on local sea conditions and species activity.

## Fishing Outlook – Early November 2026

The beginning of November offers several interesting possibilities for anglers visiting San Jose del Cabo and the surrounding waters of Los Cabos.

**Yellowfin tuna** remain a major offshore target, particularly around the Inner and Outer Gordo Banks, where opportunities for larger fish have continued.

**Wahoo** activity has also shown encouraging signs, especially along the eastern fishing grounds.

**Dorado** remain less consistent, while bottom fishing opportunities depend on local conditions and species availability.

The gradual cooling of offshore waters may influence how fish move and feed during the coming weeks.

As always, daily weather conditions, currents, bait concentrations, and fishing pressure can significantly affect results.

For anglers considering a fishing trip in early November, flexibility and awareness of current local conditions remain especially important.
