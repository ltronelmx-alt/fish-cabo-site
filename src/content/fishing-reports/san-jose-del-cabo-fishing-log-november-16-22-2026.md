**Yellowfin tuna were more dependable at both Gordo Banks during November 16–22, 2026, and wahoo gave anglers a second target, especially at Punta Gorda and Inner Gordo.** Local crews reported their best tuna action in the afternoon, when northerly winds also tended to pick up.

This regional log draws on the [November 22 Gordo Banks Pangas bulletin](https://gordobanks.com/tuna-and-wahoo/) and its [November 15 report](https://gordobanks.com/slow-tuna-bite-opportunities-for-big-ones/) for comparison. The catches and hookups below come from those regional reports.

## Weather and Ocean Conditions

Water was around **83°F**, close to the cooler readings from late in the previous week, when the reported average was about 84°F. Sunny afternoons reached the low 80s, with mornings in the mid to upper 60s. Winds were generally light to moderate and tended to build from the north later in the day.

That timing matters for anglers deciding when to fish the banks. The stronger tuna reports came from later outings, so the trade-off was between the afternoon bite and the wind that often came with it.

## Yellowfin Tuna — A More Consistent Bite at Both Banks

Gordo Banks Pangas reported a steadier bite at **Inner and Outer Gordo** after a week when long tuna outings sometimes ended without a fish. This week’s catches ranged from very small yellowfin to fish around **200 lb**. Some local crews reported landing **seven to eight tuna** in an afternoon. Those counts describe particularly productive outings, and the bulletin gives no typical catch count for morning trips.

Live sardines were still unavailable in the regional report. Squid, chiwili, and small live skipjack or bulito all produced tuna. With small fish and much heavier yellowfin feeding on the same grounds, the source favored staying with at least 50- or 60-pound-test line.

## Wahoo and Dorado — More Wahoo, Scattered Dorado

**Punta Gorda (Cerro) and Inner Gordo** accounted for most reported wahoo, with Rapala lures and rigged ballyhoo producing fish. Trolling also turned up scattered wahoo at Iman, the 25 and La Fortuna. The bulletin reported several wahoo around **40–55 lb**. Some crews started the day trolling for wahoo and then switched to tuna, while others fished the same targets in reverse order.

Inner Gordo also produced incidental wahoo for tuna anglers drifting squid or slow-trolling chiwili. That was a change from the prior bulletin, which described wahoo hooked and lost on tuna gear at the same bank. More dorado were seen this week, though the source still observed only one or two a day across the dock area. Early in the week, two dorado of about **20 lb** each took drifting squid at Inner Gordo.

## Billfish and Bottom Fishing

Striped marlin and sailfish were reported on slow-trolled live skipjack at the Gordo Banks and on ballyhoo trolled for wahoo. The bulletin recorded catches on both presentations without giving a daily count or identifying a stronger bank for billfish.

Bottom fishing stayed slow across the rocky grounds at Vinorama, San Luis, Iman, La Fortuna, the 25 and Cardon. The reported catch consisted of a few small snapper, grouper and triggerfish.

## Fishing Outlook

Tuna at the two Gordo Banks and wahoo at Punta Gorda and Inner Gordo give anglers two options to discuss before departure. Afternoon tuna results are worth weighing against the latest wind conditions. With sardines still scarce this week, confirm which live baits are available. The few dorado and small bottom catches give little reason to prioritize either on the strength of this report alone.

## Weekly Fishing Highlights

- **Tuna:** More regular action at both banks, with yellowfin up to about 200 lb and stronger afternoon reports.
- **Wahoo:** Punta Gorda and Inner Gordo led the regional reports, with several fish around 40–55 lb.
- **Bait:** Squid, chiwili and small live skipjack or bulito produced tuna while live sardines remained unavailable.
- **Conditions:** Water held near 83°F and predominantly northerly winds tended to build later in the day.
